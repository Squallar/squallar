What it does

Sub-volume latency

Reads the real-time Level 2 chunk feed rather than waiting for a completed volume, so the lowest tilt lands seconds after collection. A WebSocket push feed drives it — no polling delay on top.

Every moment, and what's derived from them

Reflectivity, velocity and spectrum width, plus the full dual-pol set (CC, ZDR, PHI, KDP). Derived products are computed here, not just displayed: normalized rotation, VIL and VIL density, echo tops, hail POSH/MEHS, precipitation rate, storm-relative velocity and hydrometeor classification.

Cut through the storm

Vertical cross-sections along any line you draw, and volumetric rendering of the whole scan — the parts of a supercell that a plan view flattens away.

Six panes, linked or not

Up to six panes on desktop, four on mobile. Link them by time, viewport or layer set, or leave each independent to compare products, tilts or sites side by side.

Everything loops

Not just radar. Every time-varying layer animates on a shared timeline, so satellite, mosaic and lightning move together with the tilt you're watching.

The whole archive

Scrub the complete public NEXRAD Level 2 archive, not a rolling window. Any storm, any date the network recorded it.

Beyond the radar network

MRMS national mosaic (composite reflectivity and precipitation rate), NOAA's global geostationary satellite mosaic across four channels, and GOES GLM lightning.

Watches, warnings, and the discussion behind them

NWS alert polygons, SPC convective and fire-weather outlooks, mesoscale discussions, storm reports and METAR surface observations.

Actually cross-platform

One codebase, GPU-rendered everywhere. A native desktop binary on Linux, macOS and Windows; a real app in the browser with wasm threads; and Android. Your layout is restored exactly as you left it.
